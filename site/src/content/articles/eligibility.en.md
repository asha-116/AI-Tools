# Home Loan Eligibility Calculator for Buyers

As a real-estate agent, understanding your buyer's purchasing power is the first step to a successful property transaction. This home loan eligibility calculator helps you estimate the maximum loan amount a financial institution might approve for your client. Instead of waiting days for a bank's preliminary assessment, you can perform this calculation instantly on your mobile phone during a site visit or initial consultation. By entering a few basic financial details, you can help your clients understand their realistic budget, saving time for both parties and ensuring you show properties that align with their actual financial capacity.

## How to use the calculator

Using this tool on your mobile screen is simple and requires only four steps. First, enter the monthly take-home income of your buyer. Second, input the bank's FOIR limit, which represents the portion of income allowed for debt repayment. Third, enter any existing monthly EMIs that the buyer is currently paying for other active loans, such as car loans or personal loans. Finally, enter the expected annual interest rate and the preferred loan tenure in years. The calculator instantly processes these numbers to display the maximum EMI the buyer can afford and the total loan amount they can expect.

## How it is calculated

- Loan = (income × FOIR% − existing EMIs) × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r
- r = yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100, n = months. The bracket is the EMI you can still take on; the loan is the amount whose EMI equals it.

The calculation is based on standard banking guidelines. First, the tool calculates the maximum allowable EMI using the Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio. If the buyer's monthly take-home income is ₹1,00,000 and the bank's FOIR limit is 50%, the maximum monthly repayment capacity is ₹50,000. If there are existing EMIs, they are subtracted from this amount. The remaining capacity is then used to calculate the maximum loan amount ₹57,61,542 for a tenure of 20 years at an annual interest rate of 8.5%. This calculation uses the standard reducing balance loan formula, which determines the principal amount that matches the available monthly repayment capacity.

### Assumptions

- FOIR limits differ by bank and by income (often 40% to 60% of take-home pay). Ask the bank for its limit.
- Banks also cap the loan at a share of the property value, and check credit score, age and job. The bank’s offer can be lower.
- Own money needed = property price − largest loan. Stamp duty, registration and fees are extra.
- The interest rate stays the same for the whole loan.

## When it helps

This calculator is highly useful during the initial client onboarding phase. When a buyer expresses interest in a property, you can quickly check if their income supports the required loan. It is also helpful when discussing the down payment, as the buyer must arrange the difference between the property cost and the maximum loan amount from their own savings. Please note that this tool provides an estimate based on income criteria. Actual loan approval depends on other critical factors such as the buyer's credit score, age, employment stability, and the technical valuation of the property. For final loan terms, advise your clients to consult their bank or financial advisor.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is FOIR in home loans?

FOIR stands for Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio. It is the percentage of monthly take-home income that banks allow a borrower to spend on all EMIs combined.

### Can a buyer get a loan for the entire property cost?

No. Banks only fund a specific percentage of the property value. The buyer must arrange the remaining amount, including stamp duty and registration fees, from their own savings.

### Does an existing loan affect eligibility?

Yes. Any active EMIs reduce the monthly repayment capacity, which lowers the maximum home loan amount the bank will approve.
