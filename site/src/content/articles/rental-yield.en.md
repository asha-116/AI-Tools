The Rental Yield Calculator helps agents quickly see how much a property earns from rent compared to its cost. It shows both gross yield, which looks at total rent, and net yield, which adjusts for vacant months and yearly expenses. ## How it is calculated

- Net yield = (rent × (12 − empty months) − yearly costs) ÷ (property value + purchase costs) × 100
- Gross yield = rent for 12 months ÷ money put in × 100. Net yield takes off the empty months and the yearly costs.

Net yield = (rent × (12 − vacant months) − yearly costs) ÷ (property value + purchase costs) × 100. Here, "rent" is the monthly amount, "vacant months" reduces the rent earned, "yearly costs" are the expenses you pay each year, and "property value + purchase costs" is the total money you have invested. Multiply by 100 to get a percentage. Gross yield = (rent × 12) ÷ money put in × 100, which ignores vacancy and costs. The result shows how efficiently your capital works, helping you compare rental options side by side.

### Assumptions

- Yearly costs are what the owner pays: society maintenance, property tax, insurance, repairs and brokerage for new tenants.
- Before income tax and before any home-loan interest. For tax, ask a CA.
- Rent stays the same all year; rent increases and a change in the property’s value are not included.
- This shows numbers only. It is not advice on whether to buy, sell or rent out.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is net rental yield different from gross yield?

Gross yield uses full yearly rent divided by total money spent. Net yield reduces the rent by vacant months and subtracts yearly costs before dividing.

### What costs should I include in the calculation?

Include society maintenance, property tax, insurance, repair costs and any broker fee paid for a new tenant. These are the yearly costs.

### Can I use the calculator for a property with a home loan?

The calculator works on the total money you have put in, not on loan interest. For tax impact, ask your CA.
